# Why I reduced the communication channels
**Date:** 2025-03

**Context:**  
These are working notes about project structure, communication choices, and attention management. They are not marketing advice and not a growth strategy.

---

## Background

During the first quarter of 2025, a strategic change was made in the communication structure of the project.

Until then, several channels coexisted:

- Facebook  
- YouTube  
- a mailing list  
- a website hosted on OVH  

The mailing list had been maintained for about two years.

Over time, around **one thousand email addresses** had been collected.

This structure reflected a classical small-scale digital strategy:

- maintain visibility  
- collect contacts  
- generate potential leads  

Over time, however, the structure revealed its limits.

---

## The hidden cost of communication channels

Maintaining several communication channels appears efficient.

In practice, it multiplies invisible work.

Beyond publishing content, it requires:

- maintaining mailing list infrastructure  
- respecting data protection obligations  
- managing personal data responsibly  
- maintaining hosting services  
- keeping multiple platforms active  
- thinking continuously about outreach and growth  

None of these tasks were directly related to the **core objective of the project**.

Attention slowly shifted away from building the tool itself.

The deeper issue was not technical complexity.

It was **fragmentation of attention**.

---

## Structural incompatibility with the project

The project is small and experimental by nature.

Its core intention is to explore learning tools and simple digital structures.

Running multiple communication channels introduced a different logic:

- visibility  
- lead generation  
- audience maintenance  

This logic gradually created pressure to produce communication instead of improving the product.

The structure started to resemble a **small marketing system**.

This was not the direction intended for the project.

---

## Decision: reducing the surface

A decision was taken during the first quarter of 2025.

Several elements were deliberately stopped:

- the Facebook page  
- the YouTube channel  
- the mailing list  
- the OVH hosting infrastructure  

The mailing list itself represented **two years of work** and around **one thousand collected contacts**.

Letting it go was a conscious choice.

The objective was not to abandon communication.

It was to **reduce its surface**.

---

## A minimal communication structure

The project communication was simplified to a single platform:

**Instagram**

The use of Instagram remains intentionally minimal.

After one year, only **nine posts** exist.

The objective is not growth or algorithmic visibility.

It is simply to maintain a **small public trace of the project**.

---

## Attention returns to the product

Reducing the communication channels had an immediate effect.

Energy previously spent on managing platforms returned to the core work:

- developing the application  
- improving the learning experience  
- experimenting with the interface  
- publishing ideas openly  

The project also shifted structurally:

from a **commercial-style web presence**  
towards an **open-source approach centred on GitHub**.

This shift changes the nature of the work.

Instead of collecting leads, the focus becomes **building a tool**.

Instead of managing audiences, the focus becomes **improving the product**.

---

## Open questions

How many communication channels does a small project actually need?

At what point does outreach begin to compete with creation?

Is it possible to develop a digital tool without participating in constant visibility loops?

And perhaps the deeper question:

**How small can a communication structure remain while still allowing a project to exist publicly?**

---

This note records a **structural decision**, not a final answer.
