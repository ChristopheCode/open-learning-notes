# Why I stopped 1-to-1 teaching

Date: 2025-12

Context:
These are working notes about pedagogy, open learning, and design choices.
They are not tutorials and not a business case.

---

## Background

For several years, I ran a very small one-to-one teaching structure.
It was non-commercial, open in spirit, and never formally declared as a sales activity.

This structure emerged in late 2020, with short 25-minute online sessions
and a small international teaching network.

The organisation remained deliberately minimal:
- at most three teachers
- around five students
- direct coordination between teachers and learners
- no automation
- no growth objective

One long-term learning relationship has continued for several years.
This continuity matters to me and remains an exception I consciously preserve.

---

## The hidden cost of 1-to-1 teaching

One-to-one teaching appears simple from the outside.
In reality, it concentrates several burdens on a single person.

Beyond teaching itself, I had to:
- manage schedules manually
- coordinate availability between teachers and students
- absorb last-minute changes
- hold emotional and cognitive responsibility for continuity

This structure functioned only because it stayed very small.
It could not grow without losing its human quality
or demanding disproportionate attention.

---

## Structural incompatibility with open work

The deeper issue is not time.
It is structure.

One-to-one teaching binds learning to synchronous presence.
Progress exists only if someone is constantly available.

This model is incompatible with:
- open-source practices
- durable documentation
- tool-building
- slow, reflective work

Time spent maintaining individual continuity
was time not spent creating shared, reusable structures.

---

## Decision: stopping development, not relationships

I decided to stop developing one-to-one teaching as a model.

This does not mean:
- rejecting teaching
- rejecting learners
- rejecting human relationships

It means:
- refusing to scale a fragile structure
- refusing to turn attention into a scarce commodity
- refusing to organise my work around constant availability

I keep a micro-volume:
- one teacher
- one long-term learner
- no expansion

This boundary is intentional and protective.

---

## What replaces 1-to-1 teaching

Instead of individual sessions, I now focus on:
- open tools
- documented ideas
- reusable learning objects
- public experiments
- asynchronous transmission

This shift favours:
- durability over immediacy
- clarity over responsiveness
- shared access over individual optimisation

---

## Open questions

- How much human presence is still necessary in learning?
- Where does teaching end and design begin?
- How can care exist without dependency?

These questions remain open.

This note records a decision, not a conclusion.
